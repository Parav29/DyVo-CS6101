## Status assessment

**1. Headline experiment.** DyVo adds Wikipedia entities to a learned sparse retriever (LSR). A DyVo head scores a small set of candidate entities against the encoder's hidden states, and those entity weights are appended to the word-piece weights. The paper makes three headline claims:
- DyVo beats the word-only LSR baseline (LSR-w) when both use linked (REL) entities (Table 1).
- The paper's new few-shot generative candidate retrieval (Mixtral / GPT-4) gives the largest gains (Table 2).
- Both hold across three entity-rich datasets.

**2. Main reproduction target.** Table 1 (L1 = 1e-5 rows) and Table 2, CODEC columns. Robust04 and TREC Core 2018 are out of scope (see "Constraints").

**3. Target numbers (CODEC).**

| Row (paper) | nDCG@10 | nDCG@20 | R@1k |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LSR-w, L1 = 1e-5 (Table 1) | 52.61 | 49.22 | 69.07 |
| DyVo (REL) (Tables 1, 2) | 53.40 | 51.15 | 70.60 |
| DyVo (Mixtral) (Table 2) | 54.90 | 52.82 | 73.20 |
| **DyVo (GPT4) (Table 2, best CODEC row with LaQue)** | **56.46** | **53.72** | **74.47** |

**4. Dataset.**
- **Test:** CODEC (42 topics, 729,824 documents, graded qrels) via `ir_datasets` (`irds:codec`).
- **Training:** the authors' Mixtral-generated CODEC queries with MonoT5-3B teacher scores (`dyvo_data/codec/`).

**5. Models and checkpoints.** All from the authors' HF dataset `lsr42/dyvo_data`:
- **Init:** DistilBERT query/doc encoders pre-trained on MS MARCO (`dyvo_init/`).
- **Entity embeddings:** LaQue (5,277,580 × 768, `knowledge_base/entity_embs_laque.pt`).
- **Entities:** REL-linked entities for queries and documents, plus the authors' GPT-4 / Mixtral query-entity outputs.

No trained CODEC checkpoints were released, so every trained row is our own training run.

**6. What already existed.** The official code covers the model, entity head, collator, loss, trainer, evaluation (ir_measures) and all experiment configs.

**7. What was missing, and what we added.**
- **Six upstream bug fixes.** Without them no run could start (see `SANITY_REPORT.md`, `ERRORS.md`).
- **LLM candidate rows.** The official configs only wire REL entities, so we added the LLM rows via `merge_candidates.py` and inference configs.
- **Budget configs** for a shared 24 GB GPU.
- **Operational fixes** for the shared server (see "Problems hit while running"):
  - a memory-mapped, shared entity table;
  - a checkpoint-resuming supervisor;
  - a faster, verified test-time evaluator.
- **Reproduction record:** `repro_codec/`.

## Phase 5 — Evaluation (done)
**Procedure.** For each query, every one of the 729,824 documents is scored with the official scoring rule: the sum of `q_weight × d_weight` over matching word-piece/entity ids. The top 10,000 are kept, and ir_measures computes nDCG@10, nDCG@20 and R@1000, the paper's metrics.

**Evaluator.** The official evaluator (`HFTrainer.predict`) took about 15 h per DyVo run on the shared GPUs. We replaced it with `scripts/big_pc/fast_eval.py`, which computes exactly the same scores much faster:
- It uses the same model, data collator, fp16 encoding and corpus order as the official evaluator.
- It encodes the corpus **once** and scores the REL, GPT-4 and Mixtral query sets together with one sparse-to-dense matrix product.
- It took about 1 h per model, and saves partial scores so a killed run can resume.

**Correctness checks:**
1. **Same scores.** On real DyVo encodings, its scores equal the official formula to within 1e-6 (`scripts/checks/check_scoring.py`, run from the repo root).
2. **Same end result.** Re-testing the LSR-w 20k model gives nDCG@10 **52.19**, nDCG@20 48.56 and R@1000 68.53, identical to the official `predict()` result for that model.

**Output:** `DyVo/outputs/<experiment>/fasteval/{test_run.trec,test_result.json}`.

**What the numbers show:**
- **Claim 1 holds: entities help.** DyVo (REL) beats LSR-w at every training budget: +1.57 nDCG@10 at init and +0.52 at 20k steps. The paper reports +0.79 at 100k; our 100k gap follows when LSR-w finishes.
- **Claim 2 holds: LLM candidates help most.** GPT-4 > Mixtral > REL at all three budgets. At 100k, GPT-4 adds +3.14 nDCG@10 over REL (paper: +3.06) and Mixtral adds +1.43 (paper: +1.50).
- **Training budget matters, but not much past 20k.** Going from 20k to 100k steps adds +0.4 to +1.0 nDCG@10 for DyVo. Going from init to 20k adds +1.0 to +1.7.
- **Within noise.** Every 100k DyVo difference from the paper is at most 0.4 points. CODEC has only 42 test queries and the paper reports a single run, so ±1 point is within run-to-run noise.

## Assumptions we made

| # | Assumption | Why we made it | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Gradient accumulation is equivalent to the paper's batch.** We use per-device batch 4 × 4 accumulation steps = 16. | It had to fit in about 6 GB alongside other users' jobs. The paper used batch 16 on one A100. | None on the objective: the KL and L1 losses are batch means, and each query is scored only against its own positive/negative pair, so there are no in-batch negatives. |
| A2 | **The L1 warm-up counts micro-batches.** We set `T` = 200,000 for 100k runs and 40,000 for 20k runs. | The regularizer steps once per micro-batch, so with 4× accumulation the paper's 50k-optimizer-step warm-up is 200k micro-steps. | Same warm-up schedule as the paper. |
| A3 | **The authors' released data is the paper's data.** This covers the REL linking, synthetic queries, MonoT5-3B teacher scores, LaQue embeddings and the GPT-4 / Mixtral candidate files. | It is the official release, and regenerating it would need paid APIs or large LLMs. | None, assuming the release matches the paper. |
| A4 | **The GPT-4 and Mixtral rows reuse the REL-trained model.** We change only the test-query candidates. | The paper says LLM candidates "are used on queries for inference", and training always uses REL entities. It also saves two 100k training runs. | If the paper instead trained a separate model per row, the results differ only by run-to-run non-determinism, because the training data and seed are identical. |
| A5 | **Resuming from a checkpoint is equivalent to an uninterrupted run.** DyVo 100k resumed from step 20k and LSR-w 100k from step 60k. | Both 100k runs were killed by host out-of-memory (OOM) errors (see "Problems hit while running"). | Optimizer, scheduler, regularizer state and model weights are restored exactly. Only the order of the training data after the resume differs (we set `ignore_data_skip`), so the effect should be like a different shuffle seed. |
| A6 | **The last checkpoint is the final model.** There is no best-checkpoint selection. | Same as the official configs. | None. |
| A7 | **The document batch size at test time does not matter.** DyVo was tested at batch 16, LSR-w at 32, and the official config uses 64. | GPU memory: batch 64 ran out of memory next to other users' jobs. | None for LSR-w (verified: identical 52.19). For DyVo, upstream max-pools entity scores over padded token positions, so padding (and therefore batch composition) can shift scores very slightly. We assume this is negligible. |
| A8 | **One run per row, with seed 42.** | The paper reports single runs, and we had no time budget for more seeds. | ±1 nDCG@10 between seeds is plausible with 42 queries. |
| A9 | **The public CODEC copy is the official corpus.** We used the Hugging Face copy linked by the CODEC authors. | `ir_datasets` lists the corpus as "on request". | Same 729,824 documents; all judged documents are present. |
| A10 | **Where the paper and the code disagree, the code is right.** Examples: λ_ent sits inside the ReLU (Eq. 4 puts it outside); `normalize: "std"` is actually L2 normalization; the DyVo collator truncates queries at 512 tokens, LSR-w at 50. | The code is what produced the paper's numbers. | None relative to the paper's numbers. |
| A11 | **Our infrastructure changes don't change any computation.** These are the memory-mapped entity table and the fast evaluator. | They were needed to run at all on the shared server. | Verified: the same tensor values (mmap is read-only), scores equal within 1e-6, and the LSR-w 20k re-test is identical. |

## Constraints we worked under

**Data access:**
- **CODEC only.** Robust04 and TREC Core 2018 need NIST/TREC licence agreements, which we could not obtain in time. We did not use unofficial copies, so a third of Tables 1–2 is out of scope.
- **No paid API and no local LLM.** The GPT-4 / Mixtral candidates are the authors' released outputs; we did not call any LLM.
- **No released trained checkpoints.** Every trained row had to be trained from the MS MARCO init.

**Compute:**
- **Shared GPU server.** We had 2 × RTX A5000 (24 GB each) instead of the paper's A100. Other users' jobs often left only 4–5 GB free per GPU, which caused CUDA OOMs and forced small per-device batches.
- **Shared host RAM.** The machine has 125 GB, also shared. Each DyVo process originally loaded the 16 GB LaQue table twice (about 38 GB per process), so only one DyVo process fit at a time.

**Time:**
- About five days of compute between environment setup (28 Sep) and the midterm (3 Oct).
- A single 100k DyVo run takes about 13–30 h depending on GPU sharing.
- The official full-corpus test took about 15 h per DyVo run.
- Hence the 20k-step fallback runs, and hence the faster evaluator.

**Scope:** reproduction only. The midterm does not include extensions or novel contributions. Table 3 (other entity embeddings) is not run either: it needs five more 6–22 GB embedding tables and a training run each.

## Problems hit while running, and fixes
1. **Host out-of-memory kills (29–30 Sep).**
   - **What happened:** the Linux OOM killer ended DataLoader workers, which killed DyVo 100k at step 24.7k, LSR-w 100k at step 64.4k, and the DyVo 20k re-test. About one day of compute was lost.
   - **Cause:** two private 16 GB copies of the entity table per process, plus copy-on-write growth in 16 forked dataloader workers, next to other users' jobs.
   - **Fix:** the table is now memory-mapped and shared (`lsr/models/entity_emb.py`), and runs use 4 dataloader workers.
2. **Stale scratch directory crash at test time.** Fixed in `lsr/trainer/__init__.py`, which now cleans up the two scratch paths independently.
3. **CUDA OOM from other users' jobs.**
   - **Fix:** `scripts/big_pc/supervise_train.sh` restarts from the newest checkpoint.
   - **GPU choice:** it picks the GPU with the most free memory and the largest batch that fits, adjusting gradient accumulation so the effective batch stays 16.
   - **Testing:** it runs the fast evaluator automatically when training finishes.
4. **Slow official evaluation (~15 h per DyVo run).** Replaced by the verified fast evaluator (Phase 5).

All of these are infrastructure fixes; none changes the model, the loss or the scoring.

## Discrepancy analysis
Our 100k DyVo rows are within 0.4 nDCG@10 points of the paper, inside the noise expected from these sources:
1. **Single run, 42 queries.** Run-to-run variance is about ±1 point.
2. **Numerical non-determinism.** fp16 and different GPU kernels (A5000 vs A100).
3. **Resumed training.** The data order changed after the resume (A5).
4. **Test batch padding effect on DyVo entity scores** (A7).
5. **Library versions.** The paper states none; we used torch 2.2.2, transformers 4.40.2, ir_measures 0.4.3, wandb 0.16.6.

The 20k rows are 0.2–0.7 points lower than the paper on nDCG@10, as expected for one fifth of the training.
